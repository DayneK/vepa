# Product Roadmap → RB Traceability

**Product-decision authority:** [`VEPA_Roadmap_v4-5.md`](../../../VEPA_Roadmap_v4-5.md) is the accepted product roadmap. Its numbered sections, phase exit criteria, exclusions, and final acceptance checklist remain canonical; this crosswalk does not restate or weaken them.

**RB design authority:** [`MASTER_SPEC.md`](MASTER_SPEC.md) translates accepted decisions into proposed technical contracts. The individual RB specifications (`00`–`11`) describe subsystem targets. [`ROADMAP_PROGRESS.md`](ROADMAP_PROGRESS.md) owns implementation status and evidence. A design, generated document, passing unit test, or DOM stub is not by itself proof of end-to-end or browser acceptance.

## Requirement-to-evidence workflow

1. Use the root roadmap’s section and phase as the product requirement; use this crosswalk to find the responsible RB contract.
2. Read the mapped RB document for target behavior and acceptance boundaries. For conflicts, the root roadmap controls product decisions; the RB master controls only its proposed technical translation.
3. Record the live implementation state, test results, known gaps, and date in `ROADMAP_PROGRESS.md`. Keep partial implementation separate from completed evidence and keep sections 00–11 unchecked until their complete gates pass.
4. Require evidence appropriate to the claim: source inspection for implementation facts; unit/integration and deterministic fixtures for behavior; benchmarks for performance; persistence/migration/replay tests for data guarantees; real-browser observation for visual and interaction behavior. An unavailable browser gate remains unverified, not passed.
5. Before a consequential deviation, record the actual-versus-intended conflict, alternatives and consequences, and obtain the approval required by the root roadmap. Do not silently downgrade acceptance or promote a partial RB slice to roadmap completion.
